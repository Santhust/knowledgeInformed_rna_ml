# Captions for the final figures

All six figures are generated by `src/make_final_figures.py` from the committed
result files in `results/`. The script fits nothing, recomputes nothing, and
refuses to run if a required source or key is missing. All numeric values below
are transcribed from those JSON files, not from memory.

Shared protocol for Figures 2–6: stratified 5-fold cross-validation,
`random_state=0`, `n=800`, shared fold fingerprint `9b587aa3d19a36f7`
(asserted at load time). Preprocessing is refit inside each training fold; the
test fold is never used for fitting. No statistical test is performed anywhere in
this figure set, so no significance is claimed.

**All data in this project are synthetic.** Figures 1–6 describe a simulated
data-generating process and a learning exercise. They are not biological results,
they do not describe real RNA measurements, and they do not establish biological
or clinical validity. Predictive relevance is not causal evidence.

## Figure 1 — `fig01_project_concept`

**Synthetic data-generating structure: what is latent, what is measured, and
what a model may see.**

*No numbers, no axes, no results.* A two-panel schematic, rebuilt from
`src/generate_data.py` rather than from memory, with every arrow corresponding
to a term in `generate_data.generate()` or
`generate_data.latent_plausibility_score()`.

**Panel A — latent generative structure (nothing here is observed).** The
shared latent quality factor `q` feeds the four module activities `a_A`
(structural stability), `a_B` (FRET-supported geometry), `a_C` (regulatory) and
`a_D` (weak/noisy) and the three structural latents `f_true`, `s_true`,
`c_true`. The FRET measurement process (`z_true`, `sigma`, `m_signed` →
`m_mag`) is drawn as a **separate, exogenous** block with deliberately *no*
arrow from `q`. All latent states and `m_mag` enter the latent plausibility
score; the score gives `p_plausible`; the label is sampled as
`Bernoulli(p_plausible)`.

**Panel B — measurement channel.** Each observed column sits directly under
the latent it reads out: `fret_error` and `fret_uncertainty` from the exogenous
FRET block, `reg_feature_*` from `a_C`, `pathway_feature_*` from `a_B`,
`free_energy`/`stem_count`/`structural_consistency` from `f_true`/`s_true`/
`c_true`, and `gc_content` from `q` alone as a weak indirect proxy. The two
`noise_feature_*` columns are shown with **no** parent: they are correlated
distractors carrying no label information. The 12 columns plus the sampled label
form the model-facing dataset. The latent diagnostic truth is drawn separately
with **no** arrow into the dataset: it is recorded, never a model input.

Three claims this schematic exists to get right, all verified in code:

- the FRET process is **not** downstream of `q` and does not share a
  top-level latent-module mechanism with it — the only link between the two
  branches is `m_mag` entering the score;
- the score is built from **latent states only**, so no observed feature causes
  it — `fret_error` is a lossy read-out of the same mismatch, not its cause;
- neither `gc_content` nor the noise features are downstream of the FRET
  measurements.

A run-time check compares the drawn columns against `generate_data.FEATURES`,
the drawn parent sets against the generator's Stage 3 read-outs, and the score
coefficients printed in the box against `generate_data.PARAMS`. It aborts if any
observed feature is ever drawn as a parent of anything.

*Source:* schematic only; contains no data and no result file. Verified against
`src/generate_data.py`.

## Figure 2 — `fig02_model_comparison`

**Data-only baselines under the shared 5-fold protocol.**

**Panel A — absolute AUROC.** Logistic regression (LR0) 0.8290 ± 0.0156;
nonlinear boosting (HGB0) 0.8054 ± 0.0233; plain prototype (P0)
0.8276 ± 0.0168; relevance-weighted prototype 0.8277 ± 0.0172 (mean ± SD over
the 5 folds; small dots are individual folds).

**This axis is zoomed, and says so.** It spans only 0.74–0.88 of the natural
0–1 range of AUROC; chance (0.5) and the rest of the scale are off-scale below
and are not shown. The zoom exists to keep the four close baselines legible, and
the differences are small enough that a 0–1 axis would render all four points
indistinguishable. A run-time check fails the build if this panel ever claims
"no axis truncation" or "full range shown" while zoomed, or claims to be zoomed
when it is not.

**Panel B — paired differences against logistic regression on identical
folds.** Relevance weighting is a negligible change relative to the plain
prototype, and several differences sit inside the fold-level spread.

The honest summary is that the three data-only approaches are close together on
this synthetic problem. That equivalence is the reason the later figures test
*what a prior adds* rather than which architecture wins.

*Sources:* `results/m0_cross_validation.json`,
`results/prototype_baseline_cv.json`, `results/relevance_prototype_cv.json`.

## Figure 3 — `fig03_prior_effects`

**Effect of each knowledge prior on AUROC, shown next to its own control.**

Change in cross-validated AUROC, each condition against its own baseline
(diamond = mean over the 5 folds, dots = individual folds):

| condition | Δ AUROC | SD |
|---|---|---|
| M1 engineered FRET feature (P1a − P0) | +0.0008 | 0.0023 |
| M1 uncertainty-aware distance (P1b − P0) | **−0.0304** | 0.0173 |
| M2 structural prior, LR (LR2 − LR0) | +0.0024 | 0.0056 |
| M2 generic nonlinear control (LR2c − LR0) | +0.0002 | 0.0014 |
| M3 correct module aggregation, LR (LR3 − LR0) | −0.0033 | 0.0066 |
| M3 incorrect grouping, LR (LR3 − LR3c) | −0.0054 | 0.0090 |
| M3 correct module aggregation, P (P3 − P0) | −0.0031 | 0.0059 |
| M3 incorrect grouping, P (P3 − P3c) | −0.0006 | 0.0042 |

**Marker semantics.** Marker shape carries exactly one meaning — *mean* versus
*individual fold* — and is identical on all eight rows. It does **not** encode
"prior" versus "control": P1a is a prior-informed condition that also serves as
the control for P1b, so a shape encoding that distinction was ambiguous and has
been removed. Which rows are priors and which are controls is read from the row
labels. A run-time check requires exactly one mean marker per row and rejects
any second mean-marker shape.

Three results, and all three are negative or null:

1. The engineered FRET feature is approximately neutral, and the generic
   nonlinear control shows that a small positive change is obtainable without
   any prior at all — so M2's +0.0024 must not be credited to structural
   knowledge.
2. The uncertainty-aware distance (P1b) **reduces** AUROC by about 0.03. This
   is the largest single effect in the project and it is a regression. It is
   reported as measured.
3. Correct module aggregation does not improve AUROC, in either the logistic or
   the prototype variant, and does not separate cleanly from the deliberately
   incorrect grouping.

The x-axis is not compressed to exaggerate these deltas; several are comparable
to their own fold-level spread, so none of them supports a claim of a reliable
effect.

*Sources:* `results/fret_knowledge_cv.json`,
`results/structural_knowledge_cv.json`, `results/network_knowledge_cv.json`.

## Figure 4 — `fig04_network_dissociation`

**Latent module recovery dissociates from predictive performance.**

**Panel A — latent module recovery (DIAGNOSTIC ONLY).** Correlation of each
candidate aggregation with its intended latent module state, from
`results/module_latent_diagnostic.json`. For the regulatory module against
latent `a_C`: correct module score 0.873, `reg_feature_1` 0.819, `reg_feature_2`
0.765, incorrect groupings 0.717 and 0.661. For the pathway module against
latent `a_B`: correct module score 0.887, `pathway_feature_1` 0.838,
`pathway_feature_2` 0.775, incorrect groupings 0.722 and 0.692.

The correct aggregation tracks its own module state better than the incorrect
groupings do in both modules, and better than either individual proxy (0.873 vs
0.819 and 0.765; 0.887 vs 0.838 and 0.775). **This panel is diagnostic, not
predictive.** It uses latent truth, is not a predictive metric, is not comparable
to any AUROC in this project, and is not placed on a shared axis with Panel B. It
says the grouping functions as intended in the simulator; it says nothing about
discrimination, and it does not establish that correct grouping *caused* the
predictive behaviour in Panel B.

**Panel B — the predictive effect (cross-validated).** Fold-level and mean ± SD
AUROC changes: LR3 − LR0 = −0.0033, LR3 − LR3c = −0.0054, P3 − P0 = −0.0031,
P3 − P3c = −0.0006. Correct aggregation does not improve discrimination, in
either model family.

The two panels are kept deliberately unlinked: the simulator's latent structure
was recovered, and the predictive metric did not improve. Reading the recovery
in Panel A as evidence that the prior works would be a mistake.

*Sources:* `results/module_latent_diagnostic.json` (Panel A),
`results/network_knowledge_cv.json` (Panel B). Panel A is a persistence of the
diagnostic already reported in Task 8; no model was fitted to produce it, it was
not used for training or tuning, and it is not a new experiment.

**Label placement is checked, not assumed.** Panel A places bars at explicit y
positions with an inter-block gap, so a tick list derived from a re-walked
`range(len(labels))` would silently mislabel the second block. Two run-time
checks guard this: one verifies the record is one-to-one across labels, values
and positions within both blocks, and a second reads the tick positions back out
of the rendered axes and requires them to equal the bar positions, with the top
bar labelled and every tick having a bar under it. Both checks fail the build
otherwise.

## Figure 5 — `fig05_rejection_tradeoff`

**Rejection trades coverage for accuracy on accepted cases.**

**Panel A — coverage against accepted-sample accuracy.** The margin rule
(reject when `|margin| < t`) rises from 0.7512 accepted accuracy at full
coverage (t = 0) to 0.8546 at 49.0% coverage (t = 0.20), 0.8715 at 22.4%
coverage (t = 0.30) and 0.8889 at 5.6% coverage (t = 0.40). The matched random
rejection control stays flat at ≈0.751 across the whole sweep, inside a widening
shaded band of ±2 SD (SD 0.0159 at t = 0.20 and 0.0633 at t = 0.40). The control
matters: accepted
accuracy rises for *any* rule that shrinks the accepted set, so only the gap
against matched randomness indicates that the margin identifies genuinely hard
cases. That gap is real but modest and it costs coverage. The last point
(t = 0.50, 0.63% coverage, 5 accepted samples of 800, accuracy 0.800) is shown
rather than hidden: it is a handful of samples and unstable, and the accuracy
falls back.

**Panel B — error among accepted vs rejected.** Rejected candidates are far
harder at every threshold (0.49 vs 0.21 at t = 0.05; 0.35 vs 0.15 at t = 0.20),
which is the intended behaviour of the margin and the direct justification for
allowing abstention at all.

No threshold is selected or recommended: these are selective metrics, they are
not comparable with full-coverage model accuracy, and the trade-off curve is
where the decision would be made with a real cost function, not here.

*Source:* `results/rejection_cv.json`.

**Axis convention is verified against the drawn data.** Panel A plots
**coverage on x and accepted-sample accuracy on y** — the convention a
coverage–accuracy trade-off curve uses. Getting these swapped is the natural
error here (matplotlib accepts either silently), so a run-time check reads the
coordinates back out of the artists that were actually drawn and matches them
against known points from `results/rejection_cv.json`: at t = 0 the point must be
x = 1.0000, y = 0.7512; at t = 0.20 it must be x = 0.4900, y = 0.8546; at
t = 0.40 it must be x = 0.0563, y = 0.8889. It applies the same test to the
matched-randomness control (which must use the same convention, not the
transpose), and checks that the full-coverage reference is a **horizontal**
line at the baseline accuracy. A transposed figure fails the build.

## Figure 6 — `fig06_robustness`

**Robustness of frozen models to degraded FRET-like evidence.**

Models were trained once on the original folds and evaluated on progressively
noisier held-out folds at the predeclared multipliers (1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0, 5.0);
nothing was retuned. Only FRET-like columns were perturbed
(`fret_error' = |fret_error − delta|`, `fret_uncertainty' = λ · fret_uncertainty`);
labels, latent states, structural and network features, folds, hyperparameters,
prototype temperature, prior strength and module grouping were untouched.

**Panel A — absolute AUROC; Panel B — change vs each model's own baseline.**
Every point on both panels is the cross-validated mean over the same 5 folds as
every other figure in this set, re-evaluated on a noise-perturbed copy of the
held-out fold — it is not a single run and not a fresh training loop. At
multiplier 5.0: LR0 −0.0293, P0 −0.0088, P2 −0.0063, P3 −0.0103, P1b
**+0.0255**.

- LR0 degrades most; the prototype P0 degrades about a third as much.
- P2 and P3 show no clear indirect protection from their structural or network
  priors under FRET noise.
- P1b **rises**, non-monotonically, from 0.7972 at baseline. This is not
  robustness. Increasing the measured uncertainty weakens an inverse-variance
  weighting that was already too aggressive at baseline, so the perturbation
  partially removes a model defect. The non-monotonic path is plotted as
  measured and is not smoothed.

The P1b curve is the reason this figure is worth showing: the *worst* model in
Figure 3 (P1b, −0.0304) is the one that improves under degradation, which is a
direct caution against reading a robustness curve as evidence that a prior
helps.

*Source:* `results/robustness_cv.json`.

## What this figure set does not show

- No biological or clinical validation. The data are synthetic.
- No statistical tests, and therefore no significance claims, anywhere.
- No claim that the latent-truth diagnostic (Figure 4A) causes the predictive
  behaviour (Figure 4B).
- No claim about corruption of structural or network features; only FRET-like
  degradation was tested.
- No selected or recommended rejection threshold.
- A prototype-interpretability figure was considered and deliberately omitted,
  to keep this set to six figures.
